Curation, Creation, and Making Something Beautiful from Something Painful


This is a small sample of art from the past few years. They serve as personal prompts of time and place for my critical thinking about art, memory, and what I value beyond what should be painfully obvious (appreciation for family, human connection, love, doing good in the world, etc.) These days, it feels like you have to be a little too specific to even speak or write anything. 


If you come to the table with questions, not comments, good. 


I tried to keep my selections to the realm of art as message. I think I probably have a zillion questions about each picture myself.


The most beautiful part of this is that each thing serves as an access point to memory when I was put in a situation that forced me to question my own memory, gaslit beyond belief by people around me, and was still somehow able to keep enough pieces to remind myself that I built while others were trying to destroy. I stayed true to myself, while my personal character was assaulted from every angle and I was put in difficult situations meant to make me look bad because I told the truth.

dollar-store-grave-tombstone-light-art-installation-bedroom

In my little historic farm house, there is so much fun light! But in one room, there is an old window that was closed off when they added a new section to the building. I often think about how the open window also faces the closed window in stark contrast. It is a useful and symbolic way to think about open and closed systems, something I care very deeply about. The flowers are poked into tiny holes I drilled. There are clouds of silver glitter that are raining at the top (probably not visible in the photograph.) It looks oddly like a tombstone when lit from underneath.
